# BikeSpeed

A fullscreen iPhone speedometer for your bike, styled after 80s-car analog dashboards. Mount your phone on the handlebar, open the app, and ride — no setup, no accounts, no distractions.

## Features

- **Analog speed gauge** — a live GPS-driven needle on a 270° dial, with a configurable max speed and a red danger zone near the top
- **Digital speed readout** — exact current speed alongside the gauge
- **Direction of travel** — an arrow plus compass letters (N, NE, E, SE, S, SW, W, NW) showing which way you're heading
- **Trip stats** — average speed, distance traveled, altitude, and GPS position (latitude/longitude), all updating live
- **Trip controls** — Start, Pause, and Reset, so you can pause at traffic lights without losing your average, and reset cleanly for a new ride
- **Settings** — set your own max gauge speed and switch between metric (km/h) and imperial (mph) units
- **Built for the handlebar** — runs fullscreen with the status bar and home indicator hidden, and keeps the screen from sleeping while you ride
- **English and Norwegian** — the app follows your iPhone's language setting automatically

## Getting started

1. Mount your iPhone on your handlebar with a case/mount that keeps the screen visible and portrait-oriented.
2. Open BikeSpeed and allow location access when prompted — this is required for the speedometer, GPS position, and direction indicator to work.
3. Open the gear icon in the top-right corner to set your preferred max gauge speed and units (metric/imperial) before you start riding.
4. Tap **Start** to begin tracking a trip. Average speed and distance begin accumulating from this point.
5. Tap **Pause** at stops (e.g. traffic lights) to keep your average speed accurate — pausing doesn't reset your stats. Tap the button again to resume.
6. Tap **Reset** to clear the trip and start fresh. Reset is only available when the trip is idle or paused, so you can't accidentally lose an in-progress ride.

## Requirements

- iPhone running iOS 17 or later
- Location Services enabled for BikeSpeed (When In Use)
- A GPS signal — speed, direction, and position accuracy depend on GPS reception, so results may be less accurate indoors or in dense urban areas
